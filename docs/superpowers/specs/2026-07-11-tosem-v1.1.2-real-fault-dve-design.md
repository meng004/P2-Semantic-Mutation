# TOSEM v1.1.2 Real-Fault Decision-Value Experiment Design

> Date: 2026-07-11  
> Status: proposed written specification for author review  
> Supersedes at the design level: v1.1.1 DVE plan; it does not freeze or amend the preregistration until the implementation tasks are completed and audited.  
> Target venue: ACM Transactions on Software Engineering and Methodology (TOSEM)

## 1. Editorial objective

Version 1.1.2 moves the paper's confirmatory evidence from a held-out pool of newly generated semantic mutants to a held-out pool of independently admitted real historical semantic defects. The central question becomes:

> Does SMS/residual-fault guidance select, from a frozen program-specific MR catalogue and under the same budget, an MR portfolio that detects more previously unseen real semantic fault families than a strong classical-mutation-guided portfolio?

The design must support this claim without relying on MR-detectability as an admission criterion, without using defect information to construct MRs, and without treating the existing P3 or Defect4MR results as new confirmation.

The paper remains a metric/construct-and-decision-value paper. It does not claim that SMS exhausts the true fault domain, that every semantic defect is MR-detectable, or that the new evaluation collection is a general-purpose benchmark contribution.

## 2. Decisions fixed by v1.1.2

1. **The existing P3 and Defect4MR data are development evidence.** They may calibrate instruments, taxonomies, costs, simulations, and candidate strategies. They cannot enter the new confirmatory holdout.
2. **New real historical defects are the confirmatory outcome domain.** Admission uses provenance, reproducibility, and semantic-violation evidence only. MR detection is never an admission or exclusion criterion.
3. **S1 versus S2 is the only confirmatory primary comparison.** S1 versus S3 is secondary and interpreted as a mixture of potency and guidance. S4 is a sanity floor.
4. **The primary endpoint is real-fault family detection, RFDS.** SMS remains a dev-side diagnostic and guidance signal; it is not redefined as a real-fault detection rate.
5. **DVE-T is not a confirmatory arm.** Cross-program transfer remains an exploratory secondary analysis and cannot support the headline decision-value claim.
6. **The primary analysis is estimation-led.** Confirmation requires a one-sided lower confidence bound above zero. Practical importance requires the lower bound above the frozen MID. The exact PUT-level sign-flip is retained only as a sensitivity analysis with its symmetry assumption stated; it is not called a randomization test.
7. **The conservative recruitment target is 20 analyzable projects and 80 new holdout real-fault families.** At least 17 projects and 60 holdout families are a go/no-go floor, not an alternative success target.

## 3. Evidence partitions

### 3.1 D0: legacy development and calibration evidence

D0 contains:

- the 12 existing P3 PUTs, 292 semantic mutants, 60 cells, and associated SMS/LRCA results;
- the Defect4MR v1.0.0 archived release (DOI `10.5281/zenodo.21203424`), including 35 `verified_full` defects across 20 projects;
- the frozen 34-case, 1,124-applied-mutant four-group study;
- the Defect4MR candidate, rejection, reproduction, and real-defect-face ledgers when used internally for feasibility analysis;
- all v1.1.1 power simulations and synthetic DVE rehearsals.

D0 may be used to:

- freeze the meta-pattern and MR-family taxonomy;
- develop program-specific MR catalogue procedures;
- estimate execution cost and plausible effect-size grids;
- develop and test S1--S4, RFDS, bootstrap, and reporting machinery;
- identify known threats, counterexamples, and platform constraints;
- serve as positive controls and explicitly labelled exploratory evidence.

D0 may not be used to:

- populate the v1.1.2 confirmatory holdout;
- estimate the new real-fault detection rate as if it were unbiased;
- support a general claim from Defect4MR's `T1 DETECT 34/34`, because `verified_full` requires an MR-discriminating oracle;
- select only programs or defects on which the proposed S1 portfolio is known to succeed.

### 3.2 D1: new MR-free semantic development domain

For each target project, a new semantic development pool is constructed from public specifications, API contracts, numerical properties, domain constraints, and FMEA-style analysis. It is independent of the new real-fault holdout and contains no issue, patch, reproducer, or Fault Card information from that holdout.

Requirements:

- at least eight certified semantic development families per analyzable project;
- at least 160 certified semantic development families over 20 projects;
- family IDs nested in project: `(project, specification-source, mechanism/template cluster)`;
- all instances from the same source/mechanism/template family stay together;
- A/B/C evidence enters the primary dev signal; D evidence is sensitivity-only;
- failures, rejected candidates, and uncertain cases remain in the ledger.

D1 supplies SMS, residual families, and the S1 selection signal. D1 is never counted as real-fault evidence.

### 3.3 D2: new real-fault confirmatory holdout

D2 contains newly screened real historical semantic defects from open-source scientific/numerical/data-processing projects. The Defect4MR upstream repositories form the initial search frame; additional repositories may be added only through frozen project-level eligibility criteria.

Each admitted fault family must have:

- a public issue, pull request, commit, release note, or equivalent traceable provenance;
- an identified buggy revision and fixed revision;
- a reproducible witness or a qualified independent semantic certificate;
- evidence that the fixed revision removes the stated semantic violation;
- a family assignment made without any MR execution result;
- no overlap with a D0 Defect4MR case, patch clone, issue clone, or derived template family;
- a recorded outcome even when every MR misses it.

D2 is encrypted or access-controlled by the holdout custodian. MR designers, strategy developers, and the unblinding analyst do not see D2 issue text, patches, reproducers, Fault Cards, mechanism labels, or family distribution before portfolio hashes are sealed.

### 3.4 D3: exploratory transfer and positive-control evidence

D3 contains:

- the existing Defect4MR verified cases as selection-conditioned positive controls;
- leave-project-out or target-distribution-blind transfer analyses;
- platform-gated cases and compile-time-only cases;
- Tier-2 human MR revision experiments.

D3 is secondary, estimation-only, and excluded from the primary decision rule.

## 4. Target-project sampling frame

A project is eligible before any D2 defect is inspected when it satisfies all of the following:

1. open-source provenance and a stable, pin-able revision history;
2. a documented numerical, scientific, data-processing, or algorithmic semantic contract;
3. a testable input/output or state-transition interface for MR execution;
4. a reproducible build in an obtainable environment;
5. a historical issue/patch stream sufficiently rich for blinded screening;
6. at least 12 valid program-specific MR candidates after original-program false-positive screening;
7. an S2 syntactic pool with at least 20 `R0`-surviving first-order mutants spanning at least three operator families.

Project eligibility is checked before D2 is opened. A project that fails item 6 or 7 cannot enter the confirmatory statistic and must be replaced before holdout unblinding.

The target is 20 analyzable projects. No single project may contribute more than 20% of D2 holdout families. The design targets four D2 families per project; projects with fewer than two independently admitted D2 families are not primary analysis units.

## 5. Real-fault screening and anti-selection protocol

### 5.1 Screening frame

For each target repository, the corpus team records a frozen search window, issue/PR query, labels, file/module scope, and screening date. The search frame is retained even when it yields no admissible fault.

### 5.2 Inclusion

A candidate enters D2 when it is a real software defect, violates a stated or independently defensible semantic property, has adequate provenance, and meets the two-arm or certificate standard. Inclusion does not depend on whether any existing, generated, generic, or pattern-derived MR detects it.

### 5.3 Exclusion

Permitted exclusions are limited to:

- not a defect or not in the frozen subject scope;
- no recoverable buggy revision;
- no recoverable fixed revision and no adequate independent certificate;
- irreproducible after the frozen retry budget;
- performance-only change without a frozen performance semantics;
- duplicate issue/patch/family;
- unobtainable architecture or toolchain;
- defect information already exposed to the MR designer or strategy developer.

“No candidate MR detects it” is explicitly forbidden as an exclusion reason.

### 5.4 Family boundaries

The family is the smallest cluster sharing the same upstream issue/patch lineage and semantic mechanism/template. Patch variants, backports, reproducer variants, and generated descendants of one historical bug remain in one family. Cross-project analogues share a mechanism class, not a family ID.

All family-boundary decisions are independently audited and frozen before the D2 commitment hash. No boundary change is allowed after commitment.

## 6. Information isolation

Three independent branches operate before crossing:

### Branch A: semantic development construction

May read project specifications and normal program interfaces. May not read D2 materials or MR definitions/results. Produces D1 and its certification ledger.

### Branch B: real-fault corpus

May read issues, patches, fixes, reproducers, and upstream history. May not read candidate MR implementations, S1--S4 outputs, or kill matrices. Produces encrypted D2 plus public commitment metadata.

### Branch C: MR catalogue

May read program specifications, APIs, normal-version behavior, literature, and the frozen D0 meta-pattern/MR-family catalogue. May not read D2 issues, patches, reproducers, Fault Cards, mechanism labels, or distribution. Produces `R_cand`, `R_valid`, `R0`, costs, and provenance.

Crossing occurs only after D1, D2, and `R_valid/R0` are independently frozen. Git commits, content hashes, access logs, and a role/time ledger form the audit trail. When one person performs multiple roles, work is separated by time locks and immutable snapshots; the residual limitation is disclosed.

## 7. Strategies and budget

All strategies operate per project over the same `R_valid(P) \ R0(P)` catalogue and use the same frozen budget `k*=4`. Cost-normalized sensitivity is mandatory.

- **S1 residual-guided:** greedy family-equal set cover over D1 residual semantic families not killed by `R0`.
- **S2 classical-MS-guided:** the same greedy algorithm over `R0`-surviving syntactic mutants. S2 is the sole primary comparator.
- **S3 MR-coverage-guided:** maximizes frozen MR input-transformation and follow-up execution coverage. It is secondary because its contrast with S1 mixes guidance and MR potency.
- **S4 random/generic:** 1,000 seeded random portfolios plus a fixed generic portfolio. It is a sanity floor.

Tie-breaking is frozen as execution cost, then stable MR ID. The selected portfolios, strategy code, input hashes, and environment hashes are sealed before D2 is opened.

The D0 synthetic rehearsal must be replaced by a rehearsal that executes the actual S1 and S2 implementations. A matched-random synthetic comparator may remain an instrument check but cannot validate the S1--S2 primary contrast by itself.

## 8. Endpoints and hypotheses

For real fault family `g` in project `p`, define:

`det(R,g) = 1` if portfolio `R` exposes the real defect under the frozen family oracle, otherwise `0`.

The project-level real-fault detection score is:

`RFDS_p(R) = mean_{g in G_p^real} det(R,g)`.

The primary estimand is PUT-equal:

`Delta_real = mean_p [RFDS_p(R0 union S1) - RFDS_p(R0 union S2)]`.

### Primary confirmatory hypothesis

`H-DV-R: Delta_real > 0` for S1 versus S2 on D2.

The headline claim is allowed only when the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound is above zero. A practically important claim additionally requires that lower bound to exceed `MID=0.10 RFDS`. A point estimate above 0.10 without the corresponding lower-bound result is not evidence of practical importance.

### Secondary questions

- S1 versus S3 and S4 on D2;
- SMS/residual ranking versus D2 real-fault detection at project and mechanism-class levels;
- D0 mutant kill rate versus D2 real-fault detection, including rank reversals;
- cost per additional D2 real-fault family detected;
- target-distribution-blind cross-project transfer;
- robustness to evidence grade, taxonomy resolution, budget, platform gates, and ceiling.

Secondary results cannot rescue a failed S1--S2 primary comparison.

## 9. Statistical analysis

### 9.1 Primary interval and decision

The point estimate is the PUT-equal mean paired project difference. The primary interval is a fully implemented and frozen two-level BCa bootstrap: resample projects, then real-fault families within each selected project, preserving the paired strategy outcomes. The one-sided 95% lower bound determines statistical superiority and the MID decision.

The current percentile-bootstrap implementation does not satisfy this requirement. BCa acceleration, bias correction, degenerate-resample handling, and deterministic tests must exist before M0 freeze.

### 9.2 Sensitivity analyses

- exact project-level sign-flip, labelled as a symmetry-dependent sensitivity test rather than a randomization test;
- exact sign test on the direction of nonzero project differences;
- paired binary mixed-effects model with strategy as a fixed effect and project/fault-family clustering, with the convergence and singular-fit policy frozen;
- percentile and studentized cluster bootstrap intervals;
- project leave-one-out influence analysis;
- analysis including all MR misses and a ceiling analysis using the union of all valid MRs.

Only S1 versus S2 is confirmatory, so no Holm family is required for the headline test. All secondary multiple comparisons are labelled exploratory or adjusted within their declared families.

### 9.3 Power and recruitment

The existing v1.1.1 Gaussian and bounded semantic-mutant simulations are retained as instrument history, not as real-fault power proof. A new binary two-level simulation must use project and fault-family heterogeneity, unequal families per project, realistic ceiling rates, and expected MR misses.

The preregistration starts from a conservative target of 20 projects and 80 D2 families. A separately labelled feasibility tranche of 15--20 newly screened defects may estimate reproduction yield and variance, but those defects are permanently excluded from D2 confirmation. Sample size may increase after this tranche; it may not decrease below 20 projects/80 D2 families merely because recruitment is difficult.

Go/no-go rules before unblinding:

- fewer than 17 analyzable projects: do not run a confirmatory claim;
- fewer than 60 D2 families: continue recruitment or relabel the study non-confirmatory;
- any project contributing over 20% of families: recruit or down-sample by a frozen random rule before unblinding;
- fewer than two D2 families in a project: exclude that project before commitment and replace it;
- audit demotion below a threshold triggers recruitment, never silent denominator repair.

## 10. Independent audit

An independent second evaluator audits:

- all D2 provenance and fixed-arm links;
- all C-grade real-fault certificates;
- all D2 family boundaries;
- stratified A/B certificates and D1 fidelity;
- rejected and uncertain candidates to detect over-rejection;
- a sample of MR catalogues for D2-information leakage;
- role/time and hash ledgers;
- the final primary-analysis reproduction from sealed inputs.

Agreement thresholds are fixed before screening: Cohen/Fleiss kappa at least 0.60 when kappa is estimable and raw agreement at least 0.90. Failure causes full-layer re-review. Persistent failure demotes the affected layer and invokes the go/no-go recruitment rules.

## 11. P3--Defect4MR contribution boundary

The 2026-07-07 boundary decision is amended in implementation as follows:

- P3 may reuse the Defect4MR archived results as D0 development evidence and may reuse its upstream repository list as an initial sampling frame;
- P3's new D2 faults are collected under this preregistered decision-value experiment and are not presented as a new benchmark-governance contribution;
- P3 does not copy Defect4MR's phase-gate, candidate-mining, status-governance, or benchmark-paper narrative as a contribution;
- the P3 manuscript and cover letter disclose common authorship, the archived D0 dataset, the new non-overlapping D2 fault IDs, and the distinction between benchmark construction and SMS decision value;
- no D0 Defect4MR case, derivative, or patch-family clone enters D2;
- if P12 is submitted or published first, its exact status and overlap are disclosed at P3 submission and revision.

## 13. Failure interpretations

- **S1 does not beat S2:** SMS remains a diagnostic construct; no clean decision-value claim is made.
- **S1 beats S2 but the lower bound does not exceed MID:** statistical superiority only; no practically important claim.
- **D2 ceiling is low:** report an applicability boundary rather than excluding difficult faults.
- **Many real faults are MR-undetectable:** retain them; quantify the attainable ceiling and mechanism classes.
- **Transfer fails:** restrict the conclusion to program-specific selection.
- **Audit or recruitment gate fails:** continue recruitment or relabel the study non-confirmatory; do not change endpoint, comparator, or denominator after seeing outcomes.
- **Negative result:** publishable only if the real-fault corpus, isolation, and artifact are complete enough to support a strong boundary result; it cannot be reframed using secondary wins.

## 15. Acceptance criteria for v1.1.2 implementation

The implementation is complete only when all of the following are true:

- the plan, preregistration, milestone ledger, code, and tests name S1--S2 as the sole primary comparison;
- DVE-T is exploratory everywhere;
- RFDS and SMS are never conflated;
- D0 cannot enter the D2 confirmatory denominator;
- the M1 gate consistently states 20 projects, 80 new D2 families, and 160 D1 semantic families;
- the S2 richness gate occurs before holdout commitment;
- BCa is implemented and tested rather than deferred to analysis time;
- sign-flip is described as a symmetry-dependent sensitivity test;
- admission schemas contain no MR-detectability field usable as a positive gate;
- the P3--P12 overlap decision and submission disclosure are internally consistent;
- all verification commands pass from a clean checkout and no untracked user submission/review directories are modified.
